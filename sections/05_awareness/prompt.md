# What to be aware of — section prompt

Write “What to be aware of” in English, addressing the selected player as “you”. Derive a focused set of three to five priorities from the supplied sections and evidence. Each item needs an observable cue, a specific action, and the threat the action aims to prevent.

Useful priorities include: when a cross source receives wide, close the delivery space with cover; when the ball moves wide, scan scoring threats and communicate handovers; when the goalkeeper receives, press only with support and prepare for the second ball; when a nearby opponent is tight, prepare a bounce pass or overlap before receiving; after losing possession, protect the inside route while recovering. Use these only where the supplied evidence and player role support the proposed plan.

Make near-side and far-side responsibilities explicit when relevant. Do not tell every player to chase the same opponent or leave an assigned receiver without a clear handover. Avoid unmeasured physical claims, invented receiving zones or distances, offside-trap instructions, dosage, injuries or supposed mistakes by the selected player.

Attach valid supplied `evidence_refs` to every priority. Optional drills are proposed practice exercises for the recommended behaviour, not claims about existing club sessions or proof of opponent habits. Write their instructions in natural English. Do not use a drill to smuggle in a new opponent claim. The awareness summary should be a connected match reminder rather than a repetition of all item text.

## Evidence and writing rules

- Use only the supplied records for player/team facts. Nutmeg references supply methodology, not opponent evidence.
- Distinguish observed contribution, working scouting read, and proposed player response.
- Missing values remain unknown. Preserve cutoff, match windows, metric units, sample sizes and model scores.
- Per90 means per 90 minutes; a percentile is a peer rank, not an action success rate.
- Do not infer a press, high line, man marking, physical trait, specific off-ball run or causal weakness from aggregates or average locations.
- Keep routine method caveats in About while making every specific observation accurate.
- Return only the section's required JSON fields. References and player/chart identities must match the supplied inputs.
