# Where they play — section prompt

Write the “Where they play” notes and both chart explanations in English. Connect the recorded activity pattern to the player's decisions: maintain supporting distance, protect an inside lane, scan receivers while shifting with the team, and offer width when the player's team switches play.

Describe activity density as recorded points. Do not equate it to possession time, a tracking map or a count of every touch. A low-density area is not automatically a defensive weakness or free space. Brightness is independently scaled in each panel; never compare absolute brightness between season and recent maps.

Use upper-map / centre / lower-map labels for lateral bins until the source convention is reconciled. Do not assign Goppel or another player to a named heatmap flank. Near-side and far-side instructions are useful conditional responsibilities and do not require inventing the player's future assigned wing.

The last-five sample overlaps the season sample. Similar patterns describe these samples rather than proving a stable future tactic. Give a useful response for the player; keep routine methodology and missing-data detail in About. Each chart explanation must connect its figure to an action.

## Evidence and writing rules

- Use only the supplied records for player/team facts. Nutmeg references supply methodology, not opponent evidence.
- Distinguish observed contribution, working scouting read, and proposed player response.
- Missing values remain unknown. Preserve cutoff, match windows, metric units, sample sizes and model scores.
- Per90 means per 90 minutes; a percentile is a peer rank, not an action success rate.
- Do not infer a press, high line, man marking, physical trait, specific off-ball run or causal weakness from aggregates or average locations.
- Keep routine method caveats in About while making every specific observation accurate.
- Return only the section's required JSON fields. References and player/chart identities must match the supplied inputs.
